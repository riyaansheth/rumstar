Can you surcharge card payments in the EU?

Passing card costs on to customers is tightly restricted. A short guide to what the rules allow.

12 October 20262 min readRumstar

In this guide

  1. What PSD2 says
  2. National differences
  3. Alternatives

Many businesses would like to pass some of their payment costs on to customers. In the EU, the scope to do so is limited.

What PSD2 says

Under the second Payment Services Directive (Directive (EU) 2015/2366, Article 62), merchants may not add a surcharge for consumer cards covered by the interchange caps, or for SEPA credit transfers and direct debits.

Where a surcharge is allowed, it must not exceed the direct cost the merchant bears for that payment method.

National differences

Member States can restrict surcharging further, and scheme rules apply on top. Before adding any surcharge, check the rules in each country where you sell.

Alternatives

Steering customers towards cheaper payment methods, through checkout design or by offering open banking payments, is often more effective and less risky than surcharging.

General information, not legal advice.

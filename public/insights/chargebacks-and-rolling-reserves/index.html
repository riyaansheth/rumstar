Chargebacks and rolling reserves

Two of the most misunderstood terms in a payments contract, and how they affect your cash flow.

12 October 20262 min readRumstar

In this guide

  1. How chargebacks work
  2. Scheme monitoring programmes
  3. Rolling reserves
  4. What to check before signing

A chargeback is a payment reversed by the customer's bank, usually after a dispute. A rolling reserve is a share of your takings held back by your provider to cover future chargebacks. Both can have a large effect on cash flow.

How chargebacks work

The customer disputes a payment with their bank. The bank reverses it and your provider debits your account, usually with a fee. You can contest the dispute with evidence, and you win some of them back.

Scheme monitoring programmes

The card schemes run programmes that monitor dispute and fraud levels. Businesses that stay above the thresholds face fines and can lose their account. Your provider will usually act before the schemes do.

Rolling reserves

Common in travel, gaming, trading and other higher risk sectors. The percentage held and the holding period are commercial terms, which means they can be negotiated, especially with a clean processing history.

What to check before signing

  • The reserve percentage and holding period
  • When and how the reserve is reviewed
  • The fees charged per chargeback
  • What happens to the reserve if you leave

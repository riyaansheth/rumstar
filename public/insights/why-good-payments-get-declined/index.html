Why good payments get declined

Not every decline is fraud. Many are recoverable, and each one is a customer who wanted to pay.

12 October 20262 min readRumstar

In this guide

  1. Common causes
  2. Common fixes
  3. Where to start

Some declines are correct: a stolen card, a closed account, a customer with no funds. Many are not. These are often called false declines, and they are among the most expensive problems in payments because the cost is invisible.

Common causes

  • Expired or replaced cards stored on file
  • Temporary decisions by the issuing bank
  • Authentication failures or abandoned 3D Secure challenges
  • Fraud rules set more tightly than your risk requires
  • Cross-border transactions treated cautiously by issuers

Common fixes

  • Network tokens and account updater services keep stored cards current.
  • Retry logic tries again at the right time, within card scheme rules.
  • Authentication tuning uses exemptions where they apply.
  • Local acquiring processes payments in the customer's country.

Where to start

Ask your provider for declines broken down by reason code, card type and issuing country. The pattern usually points to the cause.

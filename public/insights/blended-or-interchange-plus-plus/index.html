Blended or interchange plus plus: which pricing are you on?

Two businesses with the same headline rate can pay very different amounts. The pricing model is often the reason.

12 October 20262 min readRumstar

In this guide

  1. Blended pricing
  2. Interchange plus plus
  3. Your right to itemised information in the EEA
  4. Which is better?

Card processing is usually priced in one of two ways. The choice affects both what you pay and what you can see.

Blended pricing

You pay a single rate for every card, whatever it costs your provider. It is simple to understand and easy to forecast. The drawback is that you cannot see how much of the rate is the provider's margin, and cheaper cards subsidise more expensive ones.

Interchange plus plus

Interchange and scheme fees are passed through at cost, and the provider adds a stated margin on top. Statements are longer, but every component is visible, and changes in interchange or scheme fees flow through rather than being absorbed into a fixed rate.

Your right to itemised information in the EEA

Under the Interchange Fee Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2015/751, Article 9), acquirers must show merchant service charges separately for each category and brand of card, unless the merchant asks in writing for blended pricing. If your statement does not break charges down, you can ask for it to.

Which is better?

It depends on your card mix and volumes. Businesses with many debit cards and domestic customers often benefit from itemised pricing. Very small businesses sometimes prefer the simplicity of a blended rate. The only way to know is to model your own transactions under both.

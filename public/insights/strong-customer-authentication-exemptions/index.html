Strong customer authentication: the exemptions that matter

Authentication protects against fraud but adds friction. Applying the exemptions well is one of the most effective ways to lift approval rates.

12 October 20262 min readRumstar

In this guide

  1. The main exemptions
  2. Out of scope payments
  3. What to check

PSD2 requires strong customer authentication for most online card payments in the EEA, usually delivered through 3D Secure. Done badly, it costs sales. Done well, it is close to invisible for genuine customers.

The main exemptions

  • Low value payments, below thresholds set in the regulatory technical standards.
  • Transaction risk analysis, where your provider assesses a payment as low risk within permitted fraud levels.
  • Merchant initiated transactions, such as subscription renewals after the first authenticated payment.
  • Trusted beneficiaries, where a customer has added your business to an allow list with their bank.

Out of scope payments

Payments where the card issuer or the acquirer is outside the EEA, and mail or telephone orders, are treated differently.

What to check

Which exemptions your provider requests, how often issuers accept them, and how your authentication rate compares across countries and card types. The answers are usually available from your provider's reporting, if someone asks for them.

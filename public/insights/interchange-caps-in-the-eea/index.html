Interchange caps in the EEA, explained

The EU caps the largest single component of most card fees. What is covered, what is not, and why it matters for your rate.

12 October 20262 min readRumstar

In this guide

  1. The caps
  2. What is not capped
  3. Why this matters

Interchange is the fee paid to the bank that issued your customer's card. In the European Economic Area, it is capped by law for most consumer cards.

The caps

CardCapSource
Consumer debit, issued and used in the EEA0.2% of the transactionRegulation (EU) 2015/751, Article 3
Consumer credit, issued and used in the EEA0.3% of the transactionRegulation (EU) 2015/751, Article 4

Cards issued outside the EEA and used at EEA merchants are covered by separate commitments that Visa and Mastercard gave the European Commission in 2019, with different levels for card present and online payments.

What is not capped

  • Commercial and business cards
  • Scheme fees
  • Your acquirer's margin and gateway fees
  • Three party schemes, such as American Express, which are treated differently

Why this matters

Because interchange is capped and published, the remaining parts of your rate are where real differences between providers appear. Knowing your card mix, and how much of it is capped, tells you how much room there is to negotiate.

General information, not legal advice. Rules and their application depend on your circumstances.

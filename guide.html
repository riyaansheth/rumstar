What you actually pay

Every card payment carries several charges, usually rolled into one rate on your statement.

Interchange
Paid by your acquirer to the bank that issued the customer's card. Set by the card schemes and, in the EEA, capped for most consumer cards.
Scheme fees
Charged by Visa, Mastercard and others for using their networks. Many separate line items, often updated. Not capped.
Acquirer margin
What your acquirer keeps for processing and settling your payments. This is where most negotiation happens.
Gateway fees
For the technology that connects your checkout to the acquirer. Sometimes bundled, sometimes charged per transaction.
Extras
Authorisation fees, refund fees, chargeback fees, PCI fees, statement fees, currency conversion and minimum monthly charges.

Blended or itemised pricing

On blended pricing you pay one rate whatever the card. It is simple, but you cannot see how much is margin. On itemised pricing, often called interchange plus plus, interchange and scheme fees are passed through at cost and the provider adds a visible margin.

In the EEA, the Interchange Fee Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2015/751, Article 9) requires acquirers to show merchant service charges separately for each card category and brand, unless you ask in writing for blended pricing. If your statement does not itemise charges, you can ask for it to.

Interchange caps in the EEA

For consumer cards issued and used in the EEA, interchange is capped at 0.2% of the transaction for debit cards and 0.3% for credit cards (Regulation (EU) 2015/751, Articles 3 and 4).

Cards issued outside the EEA are covered by separate commitments Visa and Mastercard gave the European Commission in 2019, with higher caps for online payments.

Not capped: commercial and business cards, scheme fees, acquirer margin and gateway fees. Three party schemes such as American Express are treated differently. This is why two businesses with the same headline rate can be paying very different real costs.

Surcharging

Under PSD2 (Directive (EU) 2015/2366, Article 62), you cannot add a surcharge for consumer cards covered by the interchange caps, or for SEPA transfers and direct debits. Where a surcharge is allowed, it cannot exceed your direct cost. Rules can vary between countries, so check before you add one.

Strong customer authentication and 3D Secure

PSD2 requires strong customer authentication for most online card payments in the EEA, usually through 3D Secure. Exemptions exist for low value payments, low risk transactions assessed by your provider, and merchant initiated payments such as subscriptions after the first one. Payments where the card or the acquirer is outside the EEA are handled differently.

Applying exemptions well reduces friction at checkout. Applying them badly increases declines and fraud.

Approval rates and declines

Some declines are final, such as a stolen card. Many are not: an expired card, a temporary bank decision, an authentication problem or a fraud rule that was too strict. These are often called false declines, and they turn good customers away.

Common fixes include retrying at the right moment and in line with scheme rules, network tokens and account updater services that keep stored cards current, better authentication settings, and routing payments to a local acquirer.

Chargebacks and reserves

A chargeback is a payment reversed by the customer's bank, usually after a dispute. You can contest it with evidence. The card schemes run monitoring programmes, and businesses whose dispute or fraud levels stay too high face fines and can lose their account.

A rolling reserve is a share of your takings that a provider holds back for a period, to cover future chargebacks. It is common in higher risk sectors and is negotiable, so read the terms before you sign.

Who is regulated, and who is not

In Cyprus, payment institutions and e-money institutions are authorised and supervised by the Central Bank of Cyprus, under the laws that bring PSD2 into Cypriot law. The Central Bank keeps public registers of authorised firms, and you should check any provider you deal with against them.

Advisers such as Rumstar are a different thing. We do not process payments, hold funds or act for a provider in the payment flow. Your contract is always with a licensed provider.

What is changing

The EU has agreed a new payments package: a revised directive, known as PSD3, and a new Payment Services Regulation. It is expected to apply from around late 2027, with transition periods for licensing. Expected changes include checks that a payee's name matches their account, more protection against fraud for consumers, and e-money institutions being brought into the payment institution regime.

Dates and details may still change before the rules apply.

Glossary

Acquirer
The provider that processes card payments for a business and pays the money into its account.
Issuer
The bank or provider that issued the customer's card.
Payment service provider (PSP)
A company offering payment processing to businesses, often combining acquiring, a gateway and other services.
Gateway
The technology that passes payment details from your checkout to the acquirer.
Orchestration
A layer that connects several providers and decides where each payment is sent.
Interchange plus plus (IC++)
Pricing where interchange and scheme fees are passed through at cost, plus a stated provider margin.
MCC
Merchant category code. A four digit code that tells the card schemes what kind of business you are.
MID
Merchant ID. The identifier for your account with an acquirer.
BIN
The first digits of a card number. They identify the issuer, card type and country.
3D Secure
The card schemes' system for authenticating online payments, used to meet strong customer authentication rules.
Tokenisation
Replacing card details with a token, so stored cards are safer and can be kept up to date.
Account updater
A service that refreshes stored card details when a card is replaced or expires.
Rolling reserve
A share of your takings held back by a provider for a period to cover future chargebacks.
PCI DSS
The card industry's security standard for any business that handles card data.
APM
Alternative payment method. Anything other than a card, such as wallets, bank payments or buy now pay later.
Settlement
When the money from your sales is actually paid into your bank account.

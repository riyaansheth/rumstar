About payments

What is interchange?

The fee your acquirer pays to the bank that issued your customer's card. In the EEA it is capped for most consumer cards. More in our guide.

What is the difference between blended and itemised pricing?

Blended pricing is one rate for every card. Itemised pricing, often called interchange plus plus, passes interchange and scheme fees through at cost and adds a visible margin. More in our guide.

Can I ask my provider for itemised statements?

In the EEA, yes. The Interchange Fee Regulation requires acquirers to itemise charges by card category and brand unless you ask in writing for blended pricing.

Can I add a surcharge for card payments?

Not for consumer cards covered by the EEA interchange caps. Where surcharging is allowed, it cannot exceed your direct cost, and national rules vary.

Why are good payments declined?

Expired cards, temporary bank decisions, authentication problems and fraud rules that are too strict. Many of these can be reduced. More in our guide.

What is a rolling reserve?

A share of your takings that a provider holds back for a period to cover future chargebacks. Common in higher risk sectors, and negotiable.

What makes a business high-risk?

Sectors with higher dispute, fraud or regulatory exposure, such as gaming, trading, travel and nutraceuticals. Fewer providers accept them, and terms are stricter.
